# Later product work

The public website does not include accounts or billing. These are the capabilities a later MediHire product would add. They are documented so the marketing site does not pretend they already exist.

## Private accounts

Hiring leads and clinicians would each have a signed-in account. The public request and application forms would remain the way someone starts. An account would let them return to a conversation, not replace the first submission.

## Applicant tracking

An accepted application would become a private record with statuses such as received, in review, introduced, and closed. Reviewers would see the CV that is stored outside the public CMS. Candidates would see the status of their own applications. The public job page would still be the only place a role is advertised, and a closed role would still remove the apply action.

## Organization permissions

A hospital workspace would invite teammates and limit who can send a staffing request, see candidates, or change a location. MediHire staff would have a separate permission set. None of that authorization exists in this demo. The editorial password protects the CMS, not customer organizations.

## Billing

Temporary staffing, shift coverage, and permanent placement would eventually have agreements, rate cards, and invoices. This site collects no payment, stores no card data, and does not calculate a fee. Compensation shown on an opening is sample pay from the CMS, displayed only when a record includes it.
